# Example: Launching a New Watch

This worked example shows how the TEN:TEN skills work together to bring a new product to market. The scenario: a watch built around a rescued 1948 Elgin pocket watch movement.

---

## Step 1 — Establish the movement brief

Before anything is written or photographed, the movement is documented. This becomes the source of truth for all copy and imagery.

**Movement facts:**
- Manufacturer: Elgin National Watch Company
- Origin: Approximately 1948
- Calibre: Grade 539
- Jewels: 17
- Condition on arrival: Running, moderate tarnish, one damaged wheel replaced during restoration
- Notable detail: Elgin ceased operations in 1968 — this movement is from the final two decades of production

---

## Step 2 — Write the movement history (Brand Writer skill)

**Skill used:** `skills/generation/brand-writer/SKILL.md`
**Files attached:** `foundation.md`, `voice.md`
**Brief given to Claude:** "Write a movement history for a 1948 Elgin Grade 539 pocket watch movement, 17 jewels. Elgin closed in 1968. The movement arrived running but with moderate tarnish and a damaged wheel that was replaced."

**Output:**

The Elgin National Watch Company built movements in Elgin, Illinois for over a century — from the Civil War era through to 1968, when the factory finally closed. This Grade 539 was made near the end of that run, one of the last generations of movements produced before American pocket watchmaking effectively ceased.

It arrived running. One wheel had been damaged at some point in its seventy-five years — replaced during restoration with a period-correct part. The rest is original. Seventeen jewels, lever escapement, the particular quality of late-Elgin finishing that makes these movements worth saving.

The case it came in is long gone. The movement isn't.

---

## Step 3 — Write the product description (Brand Writer skill)

**Brief given to Claude:** "Write a short product description for the watch — contemporary red case, pink dial, maroon strap built around this movement."

**Output:**

A 1948 Elgin Grade 539 movement — 17 jewels, still running — inside a contemporary red case with a pink dial and maroon strap. The movement chose the brief. The design followed from there.

---

## Step 4 — Direct the product photography (Image Art Director skill)

**Skill used:** `skills/generation/image-art-director/SKILL.md`
**Brief given to Claude:** "Flat lay product shot of the finished watch — red case, pink dial, maroon strap — for the product page."

**Prompt generated:**

"Flat lay product photography of a contemporary mechanical watch with a bold, considered colorway. Red case, slim silver bezel, pink dial with clean minimal hour markers and silver hands, maroon leather strap. Shot directly from above, perfectly centered, soft even studio lighting. Crown at 3, hands at 10:10. Graphic and intentional — serious about form, unafraid of color. No hands, no human presence. Photorealistic. White background."

---

## Step 5 — Review the copy (Copy Reviewer skill)

**Skill used:** `skills/governance/copy-reviewer/SKILL.md`
**Copy submitted for review:** The movement history from Step 2.

**Review returned:**

**Verdict:** On-Brand

**What is working:** Movement-first structure. Specific — names the manufacturer, calibre, jewel count, era. Does not over-explain. The final line ("The case it came in is long gone. The movement isn't.") is economical and on-brand. No banned words. Title Case throughout.

**Flags:** None.

**Overall note:** Ready to use.

---

## What this example shows

One movement. Five steps. Every piece of copy and every image brief traces back to the same source: the movement's own history. That is the TEN:TEN method — the brief comes from the object, not the other way around.
