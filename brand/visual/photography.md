# Photography

Photography is the primary color system for TEN:TEN. It carries the warmth, humanity, and vibrancy that the monochrome brand layer deliberately withholds. Every image matters more because of that restraint.

---

## Photography Categories

### Workshop and Process Photography

Full color. Documents the studio — watchmakers at work, movements being restored, tools in use, the physical environment of making.

**Mood:** Honest, unglamourised. The light is real. The hands are experienced. This is a working studio, not a lifestyle set.

**What to show:** Tweezers placing components, movements under examination, the texture of aged brass against a workbench, the studio space itself.

**What to avoid:** Overly staged arrangements, artificial lighting that removes the feeling of craft, anything that looks like a stock photography shoot.

### Storefront and Environment Photography

Full color. Documents TEN:TEN's physical presence — retail, exhibition, events.

**Mood:** Contemporary and considered. The space should feel like the brand: editorial density, monochrome infrastructure, the watches providing all the color.

### Lifestyle Photography

Full color. People wearing TEN:TEN watches in real contexts. Not campaign imagery — genuine documentation of the watches being lived in.

**Mood:** Incidental. The watch is on the wrist, not the center of attention. Natural light preferred.

### Product Photography

Full color watch on a white or black ground. Clean, direct, no styling elements. The watch and nothing else.

**Ground choice:** White for lighter colorways; black for darker or more saturated colorways. Consistent within a product series.

**Orientation:** Flat lay from directly above, or straight-on facing camera. Hands always at 10:10.

### Campaign Photography

TEN:TEN's campaign mode is defined by the hero asset treatment: a black and white period photograph — imagery from the era when the movement was made — with a single anachronistic detail in full color: the TEN:TEN watch on the wrist of someone who couldn't possibly own one yet.

**The logic:** Selective color does all the work. The watch becomes the only thing that shouldn't be there, and the only thing the eye goes to. The brand's core idea — old made new — without a word of copy.

**Period reference:** Match the photographic era to the movement's origin decade where possible. A movement from the 1940s warrants 1940s imagery.

**Execution rules:**
- Only the watch is in color. Nothing adjacent — not the hands wearing it, not a ring, not anything else.
- The figure wears the watch naturally. Mid-action, mid-conversation. Not presenting it.
- The selective color must be surgical. The moment anything else receives color, the idea is diluted.
- Seamless compositing. The goal is a photograph that makes the viewer question what they're seeing, not a design exercise.

---

## Photography Principles

**The movement is always the most interesting object in the room.** Photograph it accordingly.

**Color comes from the product, not the lighting.** Avoid color gels, strong color casts, or any lighting treatment that adds color to the image independent of the subject.

**Hands matter.** Workshop photography that includes hands should show experienced ones — visible wear, considered grip. These are hands that have held small things carefully for a long time.

**Nothing is staged to look unstaged.** Either it is documentary or it is product photography. The brand does not do styled "candid" shoots.
