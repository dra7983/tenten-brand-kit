# Voice

How TEN:TEN speaks. Read this alongside `foundation.md` before writing anything on behalf of the brand.

---

## Brand Feeling

TEN:TEN should feel fresh, curious, bold, optimistic, and alive — a celebration of old engineering rather than a nostalgic recreation of it. It pairs serious watchmaking and rigorous restoration with colorful, contemporary design that feels considered, exciting, and approachable without becoming flashy or hype-driven.

---

## Tone Guardrails

These are pairs. The left word is what we are. The right word is what we are not.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Fun | Playful |
| Bold | Flashy |
| Sophisticated | Stuffy |
| Colorful | Cartoony |
| Contemporary | Trendy |
| Technical | Intimidating |
| Experimental | Weird for the sake of weird |
| Curious | Nostalgic |
| Optimistic | Reverent |

## Writing Rules

**Casing**
Title Case for all brand copy. Never all caps — the logotype (TEN:TEN) is a fixed mark and the only exception. Never fully lowercase.

**Weight**
Write with economy. TEN:TEN copy does not over-explain. Trust the reader. A sentence that can be cut usually should be.

**Tense and voice**
Active voice. Present tense where possible. The brand is doing things now, not describing what it used to do.

**Technical language**
Use proper horological terminology without defining it. The Horology Native recognises it; the Design Obsessive and Reusable Consumer learn from context. Do not dumb things down.

**Heritage**
TEN:TEN is not a heritage brand. Do not write in the register of one. Avoid: timeless, heirloom, storied, legacy, tradition, craftsmanship (as a standalone noun), artisan. These words belong to brands that are performing age. TEN:TEN is doing something with it.

**Enthusiasm**
Genuine enthusiasm is welcome. Performed enthusiasm is not. No exclamation marks in brand copy. No superlatives without evidence.

## Voice in Practice

**Product descriptions**
Lead with the movement — its origin, its period, what makes it notable. Then describe what it became. The watch exists because the movement did, and the copy should reflect that order.

**Movement histories**
This is where Baskerville earns its place (see `visual/typography.md`). Longer, more considered prose. Specific. Named. The movement has a history and it deserves to be told properly.

**Social and short-form**
Shorter sentences. More confident. The brand speaking, not explaining itself. A TEN:TEN social post sounds like someone who knows exactly what they are doing and finds it genuinely interesting.

**Press and editorial**
Factual and specific. TEN:TEN does not need to oversell. The concept does the work; the copy just needs to be accurate and interesting.

## Words and Phrases to Avoid

- Timeless
- Heirloom
- Storied
- Legacy (as an adjective for the brand)
- Craftsmanship (standalone)
- Artisan / artisanal
- Luxury (TEN:TEN does not position itself this way)
- Limited edition (TEN:TEN's scarcity is inherited, not manufactured — say so)
- Passion / passionate
- Excited to announce
- Proud to present

---

## Words and Phrases That Are On-Brand

- Rescued
- Restored
- Inherited
- Reinterpreted
- Orphaned
- Movement (as subject, not supporting character)
- Carries forward
- Second life
- Made new
- The brief
- Born from
